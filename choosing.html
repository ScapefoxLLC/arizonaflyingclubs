Choosing a Club

Clubs are a great way to fly quality airplanes without the money and effort of owning one. But they differ enormously in cost, aircraft, availability and how they are run. A little homework up front saves a lot of frustration later.

1. Know what you want to fly, and why

Start with your mission. Hamburger runs and local sightseeing need a very different club from week-long trips to the Midwest or regular IFR practice. Be honest about how many hours a month you will really fly: that number drives which club is cheapest for you.

2. Pick the kind of club

Traditional (Equity) Flying Clubs

100% non-profit, shared ownership: a shared airplane, shared insurance, and members vote on club and maintenance decisions. No non-owned insurance needed.

Non-Traditional Flying Clubs

The airplanes are not jointly or evenly owned by the members. Owners make the maintenance decisions and usually make a profit; non-owned insurance is almost always required (assume $500-1000 annually).

Social Flying Clubs

Clubs built around pilots, not airplanes: hangar BBQs, fly-outs, breakfasts and learning. They don't own airplanes or hangars.

About non-owned insurance: in an equity club, the club's policy covers the members. Most non-traditional clubs also require you to buy your own non-owned (renter's) policy, often with a stated minimum such as $50,000 of hull coverage. Get a quote before you join and add it to the cost. Non-owned insurance usually costs at least $500 to $1,000 a year.

3. Compare the real cost

Compare clubs on what you will pay in a typical month, not on the hourly rate alone: monthly dues, plus hours flown times the rate, plus fuel if the rate is dry. A club with a low rate and high dues can cost more than a club with a higher rate if you only fly a few hours. Hobbs time runs longer than tach time, so a tach rate is cheaper than the same Hobbs rate. The club wizard does this arithmetic for you.

4. Visit, and ask questions

Go to a meeting, look at the airplanes, and talk to members who are not on the board.

The most important questions to ask

  1. Does the airplane (or do the airplanes) have any significant damage history?
    • Airplanes with structural damage that has been repaired may have non-standard flight characteristics, and may not be as safe.
  2. Have there been any aircraft incidents or accidents in the last 5 years that have been reported to the FAA or the FSDO?
  3. Who makes the big decisions? Is it a board vote, or do all members get a vote?
    • Some members prefer to be hands-off, while others prefer to be involved in fleet, maintenance and financial decisions.
  4. What is the total time of the airframes?
    • Most pilots prefer to avoid airframes with more than 10,000 hours total time.
  5. Do all members pay the same monthly dues? Or do board members get a "free ride"?
    • Officers at some of the clubs do not pay monthly dues. This may have an impact on their motivations when making decisions, and increases the costs for members who are not on the board.
  6. How does the club feel about long distance trips, and what is the minimum usage per day?
    • Some clubs prefer to keep the airplane local, while others love having members take it on multi-day trips.

And a few more worth asking:

Money

  • What is the join fee, and how much of it is refundable equity when I leave? Can I resell my share?
  • What do the monthly dues cover (insurance, hangar, annual, databases)?
  • Is the hourly rate wet or dry? Is it on Hobbs or tach time?
  • Do I need to buy non-owned insurance, and how much coverage?
  • When did rates last go up? Is there a reserve for engine overhauls, or will there be special assessments?

Availability

  • How many members share each airplane? How far ahead do I need to book a weekend?
  • How hard is it to book the airplane? Ask for a screenshot of last month's online schedule.
  • Is Mexico or Canada allowed?
  • How much maintenance downtime did the airplane have last year?

Aircraft & joining

  • How old are the airframes and engines, and what are the times since overhaul?
  • Who does the maintenance, and how are squawks reported and fixed?
  • What experience is required, and what does the checkout involve? Are there CFIs in the club?
  • Is there a waiting list? Can I talk to a few current members, and see the airplane and the books?

Which clubs work for what

A quick guide to where each club shines. It is a starting point, not a ranking.

  • Lowest cost of flying: Flying Ten Flying Club, Payson Aero Club, Scottsdale Flying Club, Tucson Soaring Club, VARS Flying Club, Hometown Flying Club, Third Step Flying Club
  • A few long cross-country trips a year: Scottsdale Flying Club
  • Fast, high-performance aircraft: Cloudbusters, Scottsdale Flying Club, VARS Flying Club, Hometown Flying Club, Legacy Aviation Flying Club, Painted Rock Cirrus Club, Professional Pilot Flying Club
  • The fastest airplane around: Cloudbusters, Legacy Aviation Flying Club, Painted Rock Cirrus Club
  • Lower-performance aircraft: Arizona Flyers, Cloudbusters, Flying Ten Flying Club, Payson Aero Club, Phoenix Flyers, Deer Valley Skyhawks, Hometown Flying Club, Legacy Aviation Flying Club, Phoenix Area Flying Club, Third Step Flying Club
  • A high useful load airplane: Arizona Flyers, Cloudbusters, Flying Javelina Aero Club, Phoenix Flyers, VARS Flying Club, Hometown Flying Club, Phoenix Area Flying Club, Professional Pilot Flying Club
  • A range of aircraft to choose from: Arizona Flyers, Cloudbusters, Phoenix Flyers, Deer Valley Skyhawks, Hometown Flying Club, Legacy Aviation Flying Club, Phoenix Area Flying Club
  • Access to a helicopter or a twin: Legacy Aviation Flying Club
  • Instrument flying: Arizona Flyers, Cloudbusters, Flying Javelina Aero Club, Phoenix Flyers, Scottsdale Flying Club, VARS Flying Club, Deer Valley Skyhawks, Hometown Flying Club, Legacy Aviation Flying Club, Painted Rock Cirrus Club, Professional Pilot Flying Club, Third Step Flying Club
  • Learning to fly: Cloudbusters, Payson Aero Club, Hometown Flying Club
  • Local flights and day trips: Flying Ten Flying Club, Payson Aero Club, Scottsdale Flying Club, VARS Flying Club, Deer Valley Skyhawks, Phoenix Area Flying Club, Third Step Flying Club
  • Building time: Scottsdale Flying Club, Deer Valley Skyhawks, Hometown Flying Club, Legacy Aviation Flying Club, Painted Rock Cirrus Club, Phoenix Area Flying Club, Third Step Flying Club
  • Low aircraft utilization, for easy scheduling: Flying Ten Flying Club, VARS Flying Club
  • Gliding: Tucson Soaring Club
  • Socializing with other pilots: Desert Flyers, Gossamer Wings, West Valley Flying Club
  • Learning about aviation safety: Gossamer Wings, IMC Club, VMC Club, West Valley Flying Club
  • Short trips to interesting, less-traveled Arizona airports: Gossamer Wings
  • Short trips to Arizona's most visited airports: Desert Flyers, West Valley Flying Club
  • Multi-day trips: Desert Flyers, Gossamer Wings
