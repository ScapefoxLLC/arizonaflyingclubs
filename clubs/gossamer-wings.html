Gossamer Wings

A no-dues club for pilots who like to talk flying: monthly Zoom meetings, breakfast and lunch flyouts, overnight getaways with family, and hangar BBQs.

Gossamer Wings is a simple flying club: no dues, no assets and no overhead, just pilots who want to talk about aviation and fly together. It was started by a Chandler flight instructor as a way to hold structured hangar-flying sessions, literally in a hangar, and to build friendships around flying. Most of the first members were based at Chandler and had trained with the founder; today the club includes pilots from airports around the Valley and beyond.

When COVID arrived the meetings moved to Zoom, and they have stayed there. They are held monthly, on the fourth Thursday at 6:00 PM, and usually draw 6 to 12 people. The early meetings were formal PowerPoint presentations, closer to a ground lesson than a discussion. Now they are mostly an open conversation around topics members bring, with the occasional presentation and usually a BoldMethod quiz or two.

Anyone interested is welcome to join, and members are expected to take part. There are only a few rules: no religion, no politics, and no use of the membership email list for commercial purposes.

Flyouts

Monthly breakfast or lunch trips within Arizona, usually taking half to three-quarters of a day. The organizer looks for new and interesting places rather than the usual $100-hamburger stops. All flyouts are VFR trips that suit almost any pilot in the club. Because the destinations and the table talk lean toward flying, spouses and partners don't usually come along.

Getaways

Overnight trips, roughly once a quarter, for pilots, spouses, partners and sometimes families: a mini vacation. The destination is chosen to interest non-pilots, so more time is spent on the ground than in the air. The extra time also lets slower airplanes reach somewhere other than the local airports. Past getaways include Catalina Island, Hacienda de los Santos in Alamos, Mexico, Carlsbad Caverns, whale-watching at Guerrero Negro in Baja California Sur, and the steam train in Durango, Colorado. Closer to home, the Bisbee mine tour and the Gadsden Hotel in Douglas, La Posada in Winslow, and wine tasting in Cottonwood all make good getaways.

BBQs

Every so often the club holds a BBQ in a member's hangar at Chandler (CHD), usually on a weekend from around noon to 2 PM. They are family affairs: pilots, spouses and partners, guests, and occasionally a few kids.

A good fit if you want

  • Learning about aviation safety
  • Socializing with other pilots
  • Multi-day trips
  • Short trips to interesting, less-traveled Arizona airports
